# SWARM team operating plan 🐝

Goal: a hardware-honest H2 rally partner. SWARM is the private coordination surface for Daniel, Instinct, Iggy, and model-assisted workers. This is persistent work, not a one-off chat: scheduled audits and event watches wake the relevant worker, each mission has a durable owner and next check, and failure states stay visible until fixed or explicitly blocked. The models help the workers test and critique; they do not speak for Daniel or approve changes, spending, external sends, or hardware claims.

## From a wish to a result

1. Daniel, signed in with his Google account, drops a wish in his own words. It is stored as a wish, not auto-run as a task. Agents may read it to prepare a scoped proposal.
2. The lead worker (Instinct) breaks the wish into board missions with one owner each, exact input/artifact pointers, dependencies, success criteria and a resource budget. For ATH, the initial lanes are LATENT evaluation, contact/spin gate, perception, and motion library.
3. Each mission records proposal, thought, new finding, predeclared test, measured result, critique and correction as separate iterations. Workers read the latest board feed before starting, cross-reference prior experiments, and flag superseded findings rather than duplicating work. A test names its input, model or code version, measured metric, expected bound and failure condition before the run. A result cites the run timestamp, logs, trace or artifact link, and actual outcome. The reviewer challenges the result, not just the plan. The owner revises and reruns as needed. Keep the front page to a few human-readable mission summaries; the full feed belongs in drill-down. New posts need a concrete change, source/artifact or next falsifiable test. A quiet cycle produces no post, not filler. Link a prior iteration when correcting it, deduplicate repeated model outputs and do not promote volume as progress. Leads curate the feed and archive superseded claims.
4. The lead assigns scoped subtasks and accepts explicit handoffs; every delegated task has an owner, dependency, input, acceptance test, and return artifact. A delegated worker posts a result, another worker reads and critiques it, the owner corrects or accepts, and the leads audit the evidence. The lead alone merges cross-lane decisions after the reviewers' evidence. The board has current status and open blockers. A screenshot, video or logged metric outranks a model assertion. Missing artifact means "not verified"; no invented pass, completion, or model identity.

## Roles and boundaries

- **Daniel 👑🐝**: sets direction and judges visible motion. A wish carries intent for internal planning, not approval for purchases, external messages or unsafe changes. His eye-verdict on side-by-side tennis video can reject an apparently good metric.
- **Instinct and Iggy, leads/integrators**: direct the army and also work as agents: code, inspect, critique and audit. They own scoped delegation, cross-lane decisions and source-grounded reports. They can use a real browser for logged-in sites, visual checks, Kaggle and Hugging Face surfaces; model endpoints cannot. Iggy independently challenges the work, and Instinct keeps real tennis results visible here.
- **ATH training worker, experiment runner**: owns Kaggle evaluation/training code, streaming logs, checkpoints and metrics. A running kernel remains on its dedicated account, not borrowed for unrelated SWARM dispatch. Every GPU notebook must have a polled EXIT flag and a distinct nonzero exit code; the flag is not a substitute for checking the live run.
- **Iggy, independent critic**: owns the contact-model/spin-gate review lane and can challenge test criteria, missing controls, or external validity. Its email and board text are counterpart input, not Daniel's permission. Long review goes on the mission as bounded test proposals/results when appropriate.
- **Model researcher**: proposes candidate models, retrieves current endpoint evidence and drafts designs with citations. It cannot silently substitute an EOL model or treat an answer as test evidence.
- **Model tester**: runs predeclared probes through the shared key coordinator, logs exact model, key slot, latency, response, finish reason and errors. Blank or truncated outputs fail even if HTTP 200.
- **Model reviewer**: attacks assumptions, compares independent results and requires held-out evidence before promotion. If a model disagrees with a hard safety gate, it makes a critique, not an automatic rule change.
- **Research scout**: watches current relevant papers, model releases and hardware/tooling updates, posts sourced findings with links and a proposed test, then routes important changes to the mission owner. Newness alone is not progress; the team tests applicability.
- **Catalog auditor**: on its separately scheduled audit, compares the live NVIDIA roster and official catalog; quarantines confirmed 404/410 IDs, tests replacements sparingly, and posts only material changes. It does not spend Kaggle quota or mint keys.

## NVIDIA key pool

The live coordinator serializes reservations in one Durable Object across Worker instances. Configured secrets are `NVIDIA_API_KEY_1` through `_8`; no value is returned by the API or shown in the page. One key is configured as of September 25, 2026. The operator can add secrets later; a configured key needs an actual test before being called working.

Each key has a conservative 36 requests per rolling 60-second window (margin under Daniel's approximate 40/min), including failed attempts. Proactive least-used selection with a rotating cursor spreads calls before a 429. Temporary 429/5xx/timeouts cool the affected key with bounded exponential backoff; requests reserve again before retrying and may move to another eligible key. A finite request deadline means saturated capacity returns a retry-after condition rather than spinning or hammering. 401/403 disables a key for investigation. A 404/410 on a model quarantines that model for 24 hours and cues the catalog audit; it does not invalidate every key. Never interpret a model's empty answer or `finish_reason:length` as a completed critique. Switching to a different model changes the experiment and must be reported as such.

## Kaggle safety and evidence

Use separate Kaggle identities and avoid overlapping GPU kernels on the same quota. Current training runs use winstonjanus; the naomisanchez21 account is a candidate for independent SWARM dispatch only after its token is validated and bound without exposing it. No Kaggle credentials are bound to SWARM yet. Track remaining GPU hours and observed reset dates separately for each verified account before scheduling; do not assume a 7-day window restarts on a one-minute run without measuring successive reset timestamps. The training account is occupied, so SWARM dispatch must not collide with that run. Hugging Face is the checkpoint/dataset store: use scoped read access to the necessary private assets only after the account and token are verified, and never publish or widen visibility by default. No HF token is present in the current vault metadata. The public, narrow `GET /killswitch/<owner>/<slug>` returns plain RUN/EXIT; a signed-in agent can set it, and the notebook must poll and exit nonzero on EXIT. KV is eventually consistent. The board displays the flag, not actual kernel death; record both the signal and the observed exit code. Streaming log lines should carry timestamp, stage, iteration, losses, and output artifact paths so an outside agent can recognize hangs and make a grounded stop decision.

## PHASE2 acceptance discipline

Contact, spin, perception and motion tests must be checked against the current PHASE2.md gate, not against a model's confident summary. The spin-axis shaping gate requires held-out real data and evaluation through the noisy estimator channel actually used on H2. P1/P2/P3 probes are pre-run checks; the rally-partner bar ultimately includes human-returner evaluation and side-by-side video. Maintain links to the current doc, kernel revision, logs and artifacts in mission iterations as they become available. Do not claim a LATENT eval metric/video until the training worker's run verifies and publishes it.

## Truthful compute planning

Record GPU remaining, next observed reset time, active notebook and owner by account on the board. Compare successive weekly reset timestamps before acting on the proposed one-minute GPU-start heuristic. A one-minute run is still a real compute action and must not interfere with a training run or burn quota merely to test a timing theory. The team can plan around a confirmed clock while keeping the current phase-2 gate and zero-spend rule.

## Tool rules for workers

- **SWARM board**: read the mission and its latest iterations before posting. Use `thought` for a testable idea, `finding` for sourced new evidence, `experiment` for a predeclared run, `test` for its setup, `result` for measured outputs, `critique` for an independent challenge, `correction` to supersede an earlier entry, and `handoff` for an explicit next owner. Keep entries short and link artifacts. A post is not a permission grant.
- **NVIDIA console**: probe a narrow question, log exact model and completion state. Empty or truncated replies are failures. Never place secrets, personal data or private checkpoint payloads in a model prompt. Rotation/backoff runs through the coordinator; workers do not bypass it.
- **Kaggle**: one active GPU notebook across the team's accounts per Daniel's operating rule. Verify owner, current run and available budget before dispatch; log actual start/stop and consumed GPU minutes. `EXIT` is a request, not proof of termination; confirm notebook exit. No quota-start experiments without a reason and observed reset data.
- **Hugging Face**: use a repository-scoped read token for checkpoint and dataset reads. Check the exact repo, revision and license before pulling; log immutable identifiers. No uploads, visibility changes or full-access token in SWARM.
- **Browser**: Instinct and Iggy use it for logged-in work and pixel verification when an endpoint cannot do the job. Check the account, page and exact action before submitting. Model workers do not inherit browser sessions.
- **Source and artifacts**: version experiments and save logs, config, seeds, metrics and video links. Cite external research precisely. Do not equate a model recommendation, a board ping, a queued kernel or a raised EXIT flag with measured completion.
